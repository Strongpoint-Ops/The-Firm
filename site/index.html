The firm

What the parent actually does.

The firm is not a holding company that sends invoices. It is the contracting party, and that is deliberate.

  • 01Contracts. One agreement with the firm, however many companies and specialists the work ends up needing. You are not chasing five counterparties.
  • 02Payment processing. Billing, disbursement and lien-safe payment to trades and consultants run through the firm, with the paper trail that implies.
  • 03Recruitment. We hire the subject-matter experts a project needs — forester, civil engineer, land-use attorney, safety professional — rather than keeping them on a payroll you fund year-round.
  • 04Partner vetting. A partner gets on the list by performing and comes off it the same way. You are told who is doing the work before you engage.

Engagement

The firm advises. The companies execute. Nobody hides behind the other.

A recommendation that never has to survive delivery is cheap to give. Ours does, because the same principal answers for the advice and for the company carrying it out.

Where that creates a conflict — most obviously when Advisory's owner-side work and Build's construction touch the same project — you are told in writing before you engage, and you decide. We will bring in an independent party rather than sell you both sides quietly.

AdvisoryMonthly retainer scoped to the engagement, or a fixed fee per deliverable — a feasibility study, a diligence file, a white-paper report.
ConstructionCost-plus with an agreed fee, open book. On projects over $2.5M, a flat retainer instead, so our fee does not rise when your job gets more expensive.
Client portalA document and findings archive — reports, estimates, invoices and decisions in one place that outlives the job — with live dashboards where they are worth having. Free with an active engagement, and declinable: we will work inside your systems and communicate your way instead.

Working Oregon and SW Washington for construction; advisory and digital work are not geographically limited.

How an engagement runs

  • 01Scope the decision. One conversation about what you own, what it is supposed to do, and what is actually at risk.
  • 02Written recommendation. What we would do, in what order, what it costs, and what would have to be true for it to work.
  • 03Name the deliverer. Which company or partner does the work, on what terms, with any conflict disclosed up front.
  • 04Execute and report. Progress against the plan, in writing, at a cadence you can forward to a lender or a board.
  • 05Close and hand over. A file your next manager can pick up cold.
